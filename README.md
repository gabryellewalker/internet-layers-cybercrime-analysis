# Internet Layers & Cybercrime Analysis

![Internet Layers Cybercrime Analysis](Internet%20layer%20diagram.png)

## Project Overview

This project analyzes the three commonly discussed layers of the internet: the surface web, deep web, and dark web. It examines how each layer functions, how cybercriminals may exploit these environments, and the cybersecurity risks associated with activities such as phishing, ransomware, credential theft, fraud, and data breaches.

The project also examines how cybersecurity professionals and investigators can use an understanding of these environments to identify threats, recognize criminal activity, preserve digital evidence, and support cybercrime investigations.

## Objectives

- Compare the surface web, deep web, and dark web.
- Identify common cybercriminal activities associated with each layer.
- Analyze how cybercriminals exploit legitimate online services and systems.
- Examine phishing, ransomware, data theft, fraud, and other cyber threats.
- Explore the role of the dark web in cybercrime.
- Identify considerations for cybersecurity investigations and digital evidence.
- Demonstrate evidence preservation and investigative documentation.
- Apply cybersecurity concepts to a fictional cybercrime investigation.

## Internet Layers

| Layer | Description | Examples of Cybercrime |
|---|---|---|
| **Surface Web** | Publicly accessible internet content that is generally indexed by traditional search engines. | Phishing, scams, malicious websites, impersonation, credential theft, and malware distribution. |
| **Deep Web** | Internet content that is not generally indexed by search engines and may require authentication or authorization. | Unauthorized access, credential abuse, data theft, attacks against protected systems, and ransomware. |
| **Dark Web** | Hidden internet services that require specialized software or configurations to access. | Stolen-data marketplaces, credential sales, criminal coordination, illicit services, and ransomware-related activity. |

## Cybercrime Investigation Approach

Cybercrime investigations can involve evidence from multiple internet environments. An incident may begin with activity on the surface web, such as a phishing message or fraudulent website, and progress to unauthorized access to protected systems or information.

Investigators must identify and preserve relevant digital evidence while establishing a timeline of events. Potential evidence can include email headers, authentication logs, cloud audit records, endpoint telemetry, network logs, domain information, screenshots, file metadata, and authorized threat-intelligence records.

Investigative conclusions should distinguish between confirmed evidence, supported inferences, and unverified intelligence leads. A single indicator should not automatically be treated as proof of attribution.

## Investigation Workflow

1. **Detection and Intake**  
   Document the initial alert, employee report, security detection, or threat-intelligence lead.

2. **Triage and Scoping**  
   Identify affected users, accounts, systems, applications, and information.

3. **Evidence Preservation**  
   Preserve relevant email, identity, cloud, endpoint, network, and web-based evidence.

4. **Timeline Development**  
   Correlate timestamps and activity across available evidence sources.

5. **Technical Analysis**  
   Determine the likely initial-access method, affected assets, suspicious activity, and potential scope of compromise.

6. **Containment**  
   Coordinate approved actions to prevent additional unauthorized activity or damage.

7. **Impact Assessment**  
   Evaluate confidentiality, integrity, availability, financial, operational, privacy, and reputational impact.

8. **Escalation**  
   Engage appropriate security leadership, legal/privacy personnel, executives, or external authorities when necessary.

9. **Recovery and Monitoring**  
   Restore affected services and monitor for additional suspicious activity.

10. **Case Closure and Lessons Learned**  
    Document findings, corrective actions, unresolved questions, and opportunities to strengthen security controls.

## Key Takeaway

Cybercrime does not occur within a single internet environment. Threat actors may use publicly accessible infrastructure for initial access, target protected systems containing sensitive information, and use anonymized services for criminal coordination or monetization.

Effective cybersecurity investigations require analysts to understand how these environments interact while carefully preserving evidence, correlating activity across multiple data sources, evaluating organizational impact, and communicating defensible findings.
